# Unregulated AI: A Threat to Norwegian Values (working title)

*English translation of the [Norwegian README](../README.md). The report itself is written in Norwegian, and the links below lead to Norwegian pages. One example topic is translated in full: [TVG-06 Algorithmic monoculture](TVG-06-algorithmic-monoculture.md).*

## What is this?

An open and comprehensive collection of evidence on the **first-, second- and third-order effects of artificial intelligence**, measured against Norwegian values. The work connects well-established, interdisciplinary concepts and mechanisms to the generative technology now being adopted in every part of society.

The work will result in three products:

| Product | For whom |
|---|---|
| **Evidence database** | Anyone who wants an overview of the topics and risks in their field, or who writes reports, consultation responses and opinion pieces. Searchable and open to all. |
| **Main report** | Politicians and the civil service. |
| **Short versions** | The ministries: one per policy area, with key findings and recommendations. |

> **Status:** working draft. The report has more than 1,150 claims and more than 1,200 sources, and none of the sources have yet been checked by a human. That is why we need the [volunteer effort](#join-the-volunteer-effort-dugnad).

## Background

The Norwegian government aims to make Norway the most digitalised country in the world by 2030. At the same time, regulation is being delayed: the EU has postponed parts of the AI Act, and the Norwegian AI law is planned to be presented to the Storting (the Norwegian parliament) in spring 2027 at the earliest.

The starting point of the report is that we must act quickly and spread knowledge about the risks before society becomes so dependent on probabilistic machines that Norway's distinctive character and democracy have suffered irreparable harm. The report looks at three levels of effects:

- **First-order effects** are the direct ones: an error in an administrative decision, a fabricated source, a scam.
- **Second-order effects** are changes in how we think and act: we stop remembering, learning and judging for ourselves because the machine does it for us.
- **Third-order effects** are changes in society: weakened trust, uniformity of opinion and culture, and dependence on a few foreign suppliers.

## Why care?

AI will change the society around us, and perhaps also democracy and the values we build it on. The report is neither hostile to technology nor opposed to change. The aim is to bring out the possible threats to Norwegian society, to people and to what makes Norway Norway. At the same time, we take a critical view of the influence a handful of private companies gain globally by steering the development and deployment of generative AI.

**Topic, claim and source.** Each part consists of topics. Each topic has claims with their own ID (for example `RET-03.P02`), and each claim must have one or more sources (`K0123`). Claims are scored on five criteria before they are included, strengthened or discarded: evidence strength, Norwegian relevance, independent sources, link to values, and validity. See the [scoring framework](../metode/scoring.md).

**Now, near and distant.** Each risk is marked by how close it is: some things are already happening, some are on their way, and some are hypothetical. The report builds on what is happening now. The hypothetical is pointed out, but does not carry the argument. See [RAM-02](../03-rammeverk/utkast/RAM-02-andre-dimensjoner.md).

## Join the volunteer effort ("dugnad")

A *dugnad* is the Norwegian tradition of unpaid communal work, where neighbours or members come together to get a shared job done. The volunteer effort has five tasks, roughly in the order they are needed:

1. **[New claims and concerns](../dugnad/1-nye-pastander-og-bekymringer.md).** Early in the effort: contribute your own claims, concerns, nuances and angles, and help shape the report. You do not need to score anything yourself.
2. **[Breadth](../dugnad/2-bredde.md).** Add sources, Norwegian examples and arguments that make the evidence base larger, stronger and harder to dismiss.
3. **[Experts](../dugnad/3-fagfolk.md).** Take responsibility for a topic: map experts across disciplines, reach out and follow up until you have a statement or a peer review. This task involves personal data and is agreed with the author first.
4. **[Source and claim checking](../dugnad/4-kilde-og-pastandssjekk.md).** Check that the sources exist, that the details about them are correct, and that they actually say what the claims state.
5. **[Counter-evidence](../dugnad/5-motevidens.md).** Find research and arguments that point in the opposite direction, so that what remains can withstand criticism.

Each link has details and guidance for the task. The rules that apply to all tasks are in the [volunteer guide](../metode/dugnad-veiledning.md). You do not need to take on much: a topic typically has 15–20 sources, and many small contributions are as valuable as a few large ones.

## Openness about method and AI use

The report has largely been written with the help of AI, both to gather sources and, in some cases, to shape arguments and claims. A single author has no way of verifying over a thousand claims and sources alone. That is a main reason why the volunteer effort is necessary, and why all sources are marked "not verified" until a human has checked them.

More about the method, the use of AI and possible conflicts of interest is in [INN-04 Method](../02-innledning/utkast/INN-04-metode.md).

## Folder structure

- **Folders 01–13** follow the chapters. Each folder has `PLAN.md` (the topics in order, with dependencies), `utkast/` (one file per topic), `kilder.md` (sources used), `pastander.md` (claims) and `uttalelser.md` (expert statements).
- **`dugnad/`** has details and guidance for each volunteer task.
- **`metode/`** has rules, scoring, the volunteer guide, the dating list, a glossary, the ID register, the changelog and an excerpt of the value provisions in the Norwegian Constitution.
- **`99-kilder/`** is the authoritative record of all sources (`kilderegister.md`, `fjernede-kilder.md`, `bibliografi.bib`).
- **`00-styring/`** has tools and templates.
- **`en/`** has English translations.

Topics have permanent IDs (`LIV-01`, `DEM-07` …). An ID says nothing about placement; `PLAN.md` does. Cross-references are written as `[[LIV-03]]`.
